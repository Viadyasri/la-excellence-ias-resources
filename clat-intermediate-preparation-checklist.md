CLAT Preparation Checklist for Intermediate Students

A practical checklist for students preparing for CLAT while continuing their Intermediate education.

The purpose of this resource is to help students organize preparation without treating coaching as a substitute for independent practice.

1. Understand the CLAT Sections

Before beginning serious preparation, become familiar with the areas covered in CLAT UG:

English Language
Current Affairs including General Knowledge
Legal Reasoning
Logical Reasoning
Quantitative Techniques

Each section requires a slightly different preparation approach.

2. Build a Strong Reading Habit
Checklist
 Read regularly from reliable sources.
 Practise comprehension passages.
 Identify the main argument of a passage.
 Practise drawing inferences.
 Learn vocabulary through context.
 Summarize longer passages in your own words.

Good reading ability supports several parts of CLAT preparation.

3. Develop Logical Reasoning Skills

Work on understanding how arguments are constructed.

Practise identifying:
 Premises
 Conclusions
 Assumptions
 Strengths and weaknesses of arguments
 Logical relationships
 Passage-based reasoning questions

Focus on understanding the reasoning process rather than memorizing question patterns.

4. Practise Legal Reasoning

Students do not need to study law at the level of a law degree before taking CLAT.

Instead, practise:

 Understanding principles
 Applying principles to situations
 Reading legal-style passages carefully
 Distinguishing relevant and irrelevant information
 Explaining why an answer follows from a given principle
5. Strengthen Quantitative Fundamentals

Students preparing during Intermediate should make sure their basic mathematics is comfortable.

Review areas such as:
 Percentages
 Ratios and proportions
 Averages
 Basic algebra
 Data interpretation
 Mensuration
 Basic arithmetic

Then gradually move toward CLAT-style quantitative questions.

6. Maintain Current Affairs Notes

Don't try to record every news story.

Focus on relevant developments and organize them under categories such as:

Polity and Governance
Economy
Environment
International Relations
Science and Technology
Social Issues
Monthly checklist
 Review important developments.
 Update notes.
 Remove unnecessary information.
 Revise previously studied topics.
 Practise current-affairs-based questions.
7. Introduce Mock Tests Gradually

Mock tests should become part of preparation as students become comfortable with the basic concepts.

After every mock, record:

Score:
Accuracy:
Questions left unanswered:
Time-management issues:
Conceptual mistakes:
Careless mistakes:
Topics requiring revision:

The purpose of mock analysis is to identify what needs to improve—not simply to track the score.

8. Balance Intermediate and CLAT Preparation

A practical weekly routine should leave room for:

Intermediate academics + CLAT preparation + self-study + revision + tests + rest

Students should avoid creating a timetable that looks productive but is impossible to maintain.

9. What to Check Before Joining Coaching

Students considering CLAT Coaching Hyderabad can use this checklist when comparing institutes:

 Faculty experience
 Teaching methodology
 Study material
 Regular practice
 Mock-test frequency
 Test analysis
 Individual mentoring
 Doubt-clearing support
 Current-affairs guidance
 Compatibility with Intermediate timetable

For students specifically searching for CLAT Preparation for Intermediate Students, schedule compatibility deserves particular attention.

10. Questions to Ask a CLAT Training Institute

Before joining a CLAT Training Institute Hyderabad, ask:

How are Intermediate academics accommodated?
How frequently are mock tests conducted?
Is individual performance analysed?
How are doubts handled?
How is current affairs covered?
How much independent study time is expected?
What support is available for weaker sections?

Final Checklist

Before moving into intensive preparation, make sure you can say:

 I understand the CLAT sections.
 I have a regular reading habit.
 I am practising reasoning consistently.
 My quantitative basics are improving.
 I am following current affairs.
 I review my mistakes.
 I can balance Intermediate and CLAT preparation.
 I have a realistic study schedule.

The goal is not to study everything at once. Build the right skills gradually, practise consistently, and use your mistakes to decide what to improve next.
